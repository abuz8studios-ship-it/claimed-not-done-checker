How it flows

The kernel

One verdict you can see from across the room — never a score, never "100% done." A STATED → SOLD meter, because "built" is not "tested" and agents blur them together.

Who it's for

Ahmad first — checking his own agents and team before believing a claim or selling a product. Then anyone handed an agent's report who must decide: real, or just a claim?

They say they built something… and then I go look, and it's half built, or it's a file on a computer, or it's a claim with no proof behind it.
The check logic (pinned) — the product itself
  1. DISPUTED (red): claim states a terminal state (shipped, released, deployed, pushed, landed, finished) and the evidence contradicts it — open loop, "never"/"not"/"failed"/"pending," "claimed" proof level, or a failing grade.
  2. VERIFIED (green): evidence names real artifacts (actual file names) and gives a matching number, count, or tested run.
  3. CLAIMED-ONLY (amber): everything else. The default.

Each commitment in the claim becomes one checklist line (✓ supported, ✗ contradicted, – nothing checkable). One ✗ → DISPUTED. All ✓ → VERIFIED. Between → CLAIMED-ONLY. Line one repeats the claim as stated; line two carries the strongest statement the evidence actually contains — never invented. The meter never jumps ahead of the evidence and never becomes a percentage. Confidence is not evidence.
